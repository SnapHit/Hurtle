The record on a search term

There is no official Slope 2

The sequel you searched for was never made, by anyone with a claim to the name. The game on this page is called Hurtle. It is original, it is not Slope, and this site has no connection to Slope, Y8 or RobKayS.

That is the short version, and if it is all you came for you can stop reading. What follows is the evidence, what the dozens of sites using the name actually are, and why the game further down takes a different approach to the whole idea of a sequel.

The record, in four lines

Slope was created by RobKayS and published on Y8 in 2014. It is still there, still free, and you can play the original on Y8 right now. Y8 has released other official titles since, a multiplayer version among them, and publishes a Slope app for phones. None of those is a numbered sequel. As at July 2026 there is no official Slope 2 from RobKayS, from Y8, or from anyone else entitled to the name, and players have been asking for one on the game's own pages for years.

So the search is real and the game behind it is not. Around a hundred and sixty five thousand people a month look for something that was never released. That is unusual, and it explains most of what you see when you search for it.

What those sites actually are

Fan games and clones, on domains with no relationship to the original. Some are upfront and say in their own footers that they are unaffiliated. Others are less careful, and at least one credits its clone to Rob Kay by name, which he had nothing to do with.

They do not even settle on a name. The same idea turns up as Slope 2, as Slope Game 2 and as Slope 2 Game across different domains, because each spelling is a separate search with its own traffic attached, and a site that wants all of them needs a page for each. That is a naming strategy, not a release.

The bigger tell is that they cannot agree with each other about what the sequel contains. One describes power ups and speed boosts. One offers collectables it will only commit to as a maybe. One says the physics and the track generation were refined and lists nothing else. One is a reskin with a new sky. If a real sequel existed they would all be describing the same game, because there would be a game to describe. They are not describing a game. They are describing a search term.

None of that makes those sites bad, and this page is not here to referee them. It makes the name empty. It is a want with nothing attached to it.

What a sequel would have had to change

Here is the more interesting question, and the one almost nobody asks. Suppose someone did make the official follow up. What would be in it?

Read what players ask for and you get a list that contradicts itself within about four posts. Make it faster. Make it slower to start so the first ten seconds are not dead time. Make the track wider. Make it narrower, that is the entire point. More obstacles. Fewer obstacles and more speed. Pull the camera back so you can see what is coming. Push it in so it feels fast. Every one of those is reasonable, and no single build satisfies all of them, which is part of why a sequel is a hard thing to make and an easy thing to want.

The clone sites solve this by guessing. They pick a few changes, call the result a sequel and hope. That is what every version number in every game is: somebody else deciding what you wanted more of.

Hurtle's answer is to hand the decision over

Hurtle does not guess. The things a sequel would have argued about are settings in the game, and you decide where they sit. There are ten.

Steepness
How steep the descent runs.
Path width
How much room you have to work with.
Obstacles
How much is in the way.
Jumps
How often the track opens into a gap you have to clear.
Floor tilt
How hard the banked floor pushes you sideways while you steer against it.
Speed
How fast you travel.
Camera angle
The angle you watch the run from.
Camera height
How high the view sits above the track.
Neon
The hue of the grid and the ball.
Accent
The hue of the track edges and the blocks.

Changes apply to your next run, and one button puts everything back where it started, so there is no way to wreck the game by experimenting with it. Nothing is locked, nothing has to be earned, and there is no better version of the game being held back from you.

Two things follow from that. The obvious one: if what you wanted from a sequel was faster, or wider, or less cluttered, you can have it in about five seconds instead of waiting for a release that has not arrived in twelve years. The less obvious one is better. You find out what you actually want, which is rarely what you would have guessed. Most people who push speed to the top once do not leave it there.

Play Hurtle

An endless descent that keeps getting faster. You steer a ball, and the only thing you ever do is move it left or right. The floor is banked and it never stops pushing you toward an edge, so holding a line is work rather than a thing you set up once. Speed climbs the whole way, the track narrows, and the run ends when you hit a block or run out of floor.

How it controls: steer with the left and right arrow keys, or with A and D. On a touch screen, touch the left or right half and the ball goes that way straight away; slide your thumb further to steer harder, or back toward the middle to ease off. If a key press does nothing at first, click or tap the game once and it will take your input from then on. To change the settings, press Settings on the title screen, or the Settings button on the screen you get after a run ends.

Nothing to install, no account, no payment step. The game runs in this page.

Playing it at school

A fair share of the people searching for this are on a school laptop, which is why the unblocked version of the query exists at all. No page can honestly promise you an unblocked game, this one included. Filters differ by school, by vendor and by week, and no site can see your network from its side.

What a page can control is how much of itself there is to block, and this one is deliberately small. One file. No fonts, scripts, images or trackers pulled from anyone else. No popups, no full screen prompts, no ads. That is not a promise, it is a list of the ways it will not fail. The page on playing Slope unblocked goes through how filters actually behave, and it links to the original, which is what most people in that situation are really after.

Questions people actually ask

Is there a Slope 2?

Not an official one. There is one Slope, from 2014, and no numbered sequel has been released by anyone entitled to release one.

Who made Slope, and when?

RobKayS, published on Y8 in 2014. It is still there and still free.

Are the Slope 2 sites official?

No, they are unaffiliated fan games and clones. The strongest evidence is that they contradict each other about what the game contains.

Will there ever be a real one?

Nobody outside Y8 and RobKayS can answer that, and neither has announced anything. Twelve years of silence is not proof it will never happen, but it is not encouraging either.

Is Hurtle a Slope 2, or a Slope clone?

Neither. It is an original game in the same genre, with no connection to Slope, Y8 or RobKayS, and it is not named or numbered as a sequel to anything.

What can I change in Hurtle?

Ten settings: steepness, path width, obstacles, jumps, floor tilt, speed, camera angle, camera height, and two colour controls. Changes take effect on your next run and one button resets them.

Does it cost anything, or need an account?

No. It runs in the browser on this page. Nothing to install, no sign up, no payment step.
